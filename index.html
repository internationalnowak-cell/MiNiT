01 — The thesis
Markets are
inefficient.
That is the edge.

Bookmakers set prices based on public sentiment, liability management and volume — not purely on probability. Where their model is wrong, an informed participant with superior data holds a structural advantage. This is no different from equity mispricing.

Expected Value = (Prob x Odds) - 1
02 — The discipline
Process is the
only moat.

Short-term results are noise. The investor's advantage emerges only across large sample sizes. A losing month with correct process is success. A winning month with incorrect process is a liability. Discipline separates participants from professionals.

Track every decision. Judge the process, not the outcome.
03 — The risk
Variance will
test you.

Even a 5% edge produces losing runs of 20, 30, 50 bets. Without understanding variance, a sound strategy gets abandoned at exactly the wrong moment. Kelly Criterion, bankroll structuring and drawdown limits exist for one reason: to keep you in the game.

Ruin is permanent. Drawdown is temporary.

The investment framework

// four pillars
Step 01
Find the edge

Build or use a probability model that outperforms the market. This is your alpha. Without it, nothing else matters. Sources: statistical models, market inefficiencies, line movement analysis, CLV patterns.

Step 02
Size correctly

Apply fractional Kelly Criterion to determine stake. Never more than 2-3% of bankroll on any single position. Variance is your enemy. Ruin is permanent. Drawdown is survivable.

Step 03
Execute without emotion

Place bets at the best available price, immediately. Beat the closing line. Every delay is value leak. Psychological discipline is what separates theoretical edge from realised returns.

Step 04
Track everything

Log every bet: market, odds, closing line, stake, result. Review monthly. Identify if your edge is real or luck. The data will tell you what no result-based intuition ever could.
