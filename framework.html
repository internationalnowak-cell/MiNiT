The Framework

Four steps to betting
like an investor.

Every professional system — whether equities, commodities or probability markets — follows the same logic. Find mispricing. Size correctly. Execute without emotion. Measure everything.

Step 01
Find the edge

Edge means a positive Expected Value — a bet where the true probability exceeds the implied probability in the odds. Without edge, staking plans, bankroll management and execution are irrelevant. You are simply losing more efficiently.

EV = (True Probability × Decimal Odds) − 1

Sources of edge: statistical models that outperform the market, line movement analysis, closing line value patterns, market inefficiencies in niche leagues or obscure markets where bookmaker attention is lowest.

Step 02
Size correctly

Position sizing is where most investors — in any asset class — fail. Too large and a variance-driven losing run causes ruin before the edge can express itself. Too small and the returns are negligible. Kelly Criterion solves this mathematically.

Kelly % = (Edge / Odds) × Fraction

In practice: use fractional Kelly (25–50%). Never exceed 2–3% of bankroll on a single bet. Set a drawdown limit — if bankroll falls 30%, pause and review. Ruin is the only unrecoverable state.

Step 03
Execute without emotion

Closing Line Value — the difference between the price you took and the final market price — is the single most reliable indicator of long-term profitability. Beat the closing line consistently and profit follows. Miss it consistently and your edge evaporates.

CLV = (Your Odds / Closing Odds) − 1

This means: bet immediately when value is identified. Do not wait for more information. Do not reconsider. Every minute of delay is value leak as the market corrects toward fair price.

Step 04
Track everything

Without data there is no feedback loop. Without a feedback loop there is no learning. Log every bet: market, price taken, closing price, stake, result, ROI. Review monthly. The spreadsheet will tell you things your intuition never will.

Minimum 500 bets before ROI data is statistically meaningful.

What to track: date, sport, market, bookmaker, odds taken, closing odds, CLV, stake, result, running bankroll, running ROI. Review edge by market type, by bookmaker, by stake range.

The gambler
× Bets on feelings, form or narrative
× Judges success by last week's result
× No staking plan — chases losses
× Cannot define or measure edge
× Abandons strategy when it struggles
The investor
+ Bets only where EV is positive
+ Judges success by CLV over 500+ bets
+ Uses fractional Kelly — never risks ruin
+ Quantifies edge, tracks ROI monthly
+ Trusts process, not single outcomes
