Glossary

The language of
serious bettors.

Every discipline has a vocabulary. Mastering these terms is the first step toward thinking clearly about markets, probability and edge.

CDE HIK LOR SV
C
Closing Line Value (CLV)

The difference between the odds you took and the final odds at market close. Consistently beating the closing line is the strongest indicator of long-term profitability.

CLV = (Your Odds / Closing Odds) − 1
Closing Odds

The final price available on a market before the event begins. Closing odds represent the most efficient market price — the benchmark against which all earlier prices are measured.

D
Drawdown

The reduction in bankroll from a peak to a subsequent trough. Every profitable strategy experiences drawdowns. A 50% drawdown requires a 100% gain to recover.

E
Expected Value (EV)

The average outcome of a decision over infinite repetitions. A positive EV bet is one where the true probability of winning exceeds the implied probability in the offered odds.

EV = (True Probability × Decimal Odds) − 1
Edge

The quantifiable advantage a bettor holds over the market. Edge is a measurable difference between your probability estimate and the market's implied probability. It must be proven over large samples.

K
Kelly Criterion

A mathematical formula for optimal bet sizing that maximises long-term bankroll growth while avoiding ruin. Most professionals use fractional Kelly (25–50%) to reduce volatility.

Kelly % = (bp − q) / b
O
Overround

The bookmaker's built-in margin. In a two-outcome market with true 50/50 probability, fair odds would be 2.00 each. A bookmaker offering 1.90 each embeds a margin of approximately 5.26%.

Overround = (Sum of implied probabilities) − 1
R
ROI (Return on Investment)

Total profit divided by total staked, expressed as a percentage. Professional value bettors typically achieve 3–8% ROI over large samples.

ROI = (Total Profit / Total Staked) × 100
S
Sharp Bettor

A professional or semi-professional bettor whose wagers are informed by superior information or models. Sharp money moves markets. Bookmakers limit sharp accounts — which is itself a signal of edge.

V
Value Bet

A bet where the offered odds imply a lower probability than the bettor's assessed true probability. Value is not about backing winners — it is about backing mispriced odds.

Variance

The natural statistical fluctuation in results around the expected value. Even with significant edge, variance produces extended losing and winning runs that bear no relationship to the quality of the underlying strategy.
