By the Numbers Glossary
Glossary

The language of
serious bettors.

Every discipline has a vocabulary. Mastering these terms is the first step toward thinking clearly about markets, probability and edge.

C D E H I K L O R S V
C
Closing Line Value (CLV)
The difference between the odds you took and the final odds available at market close. Consistently beating the closing line is the strongest indicator of long-term profitability. If you consistently beat closing prices, you are demonstrating genuine edge — regardless of short-term results.
CLV = (Your Odds / Closing Odds) − 1
Closing Odds
The final price available on a market immediately before the event begins. Closing odds represent the most efficient market price — the aggregated wisdom of all participants and bookmaker adjustments. They are the benchmark against which all earlier prices are measured.
D
Drawdown
The reduction in bankroll from a peak to a subsequent trough. Every profitable strategy experiences drawdowns. The question is not whether they will occur — they will — but whether your bankroll management allows you to survive them. A 50% drawdown requires a 100% gain to recover.
E
Expected Value (EV)
The average outcome of a decision over infinite repetitions. A positive EV bet is one where the true probability of winning exceeds the implied probability in the offered odds. This is the foundation of every profitable betting strategy. Without positive EV, nothing else matters.
EV = (True Probability × Decimal Odds) − 1
Edge
The quantifiable advantage a bettor holds over the market. Edge is not a feeling or a conviction — it is a measurable difference between your probability estimate and the market's implied probability. Edge must be proven over large samples, not assumed from short-term results.
H
Handicap (Asian)
A market structure that eliminates the draw outcome by giving one team a virtual head start. Asian Handicaps operate at lower margins than 1X2 markets, making them preferred by value bettors. Half-ball handicaps eliminate the possibility of a push.
I
Implied Probability
The probability of an outcome as implied by the offered odds. Calculated by dividing 1 by the decimal odds. In a fair market, implied probabilities sum to 100%. In a bookmaker market, they sum above 100% — the excess is the overround (margin).
Implied Probability = 1 / Decimal Odds
K
Kelly Criterion
A mathematical formula for optimal bet sizing that maximises long-term bankroll growth while avoiding ruin. Full Kelly can produce extreme variance — most professionals use fractional Kelly (25–50%) to reduce volatility while maintaining growth. Invented by John L. Kelly Jr. in 1956.
Kelly % = (bp − q) / b — where b = net odds, p = win probability, q = loss probability
L
Line Movement
The change in odds between opening and closing. Sharp line movement — significant odds movement driven by professional money — often indicates where informed opinion sits. Following sharp money is not a strategy in itself, but understanding why lines move is essential market knowledge.
O
Overround
The bookmaker's built-in margin. In a two-outcome market with true 50/50 probability, fair odds would be 2.00 each. A bookmaker offering 1.90 each embeds a margin of approximately 5.26%. This is the structural advantage the house holds. Your edge must overcome it.
Overround = (Sum of implied probabilities) − 1
R
ROI (Return on Investment)
Total profit divided by total staked, expressed as a percentage. The primary long-term performance metric. Professional value bettors typically achieve 3–8% ROI over large samples. Anyone claiming 20%+ ROI over fewer than 1,000 bets should be viewed with scepticism.
ROI = (Total Profit / Total Staked) × 100
S
Sharp Bettor
A professional or semi-professional bettor whose wagers are informed by superior information, models or analysis. Sharp money moves markets. Bookmakers limit or close accounts of sharp bettors — which is itself a signal of edge. The opposite of a sharp is a "square" — a recreational, opinion-driven bettor.
Square Bettor
A recreational bettor who wagers based on narrative, favourites, popular opinion or emotional attachment. Square money is the primary source of bookmaker profit. Understanding square behaviour helps identify where markets are mispriced relative to true probability.
V
Value Bet
A bet where the offered odds imply a lower probability than the bettor's assessed true probability. A bet on a 50% probability event at odds of 2.10 (implying 47.6%) is a value bet with +5% EV. Value is not about backing winners — it is about backing mispriced odds.
Variance
The natural statistical fluctuation in results around the expected value. Even with significant edge, variance produces extended losing and winning runs that bear no relationship to the quality of the underlying strategy. The longer the sample, the more results converge toward the true expected value.
